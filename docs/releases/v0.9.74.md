# adOmnia v0.9.74 — Choose your game

The Hub arcade now opens a menu with six titles. Click **Arcade** or the
adOmnia wordmark, choose a game and play. No game is selected randomly;
**Esc** returns to the menu.

## Two new dot-matrix games

- **Dot Bubble** — aim with Left/Right or A/D, fire with Space or Enter and
  match three adjacent bubbles. Matched groups disappear and disconnected
  bubbles fall. Clear the ceiling before the field reaches the danger line;
  a new row descends every six shots. Dot, plus and cross symbols identify
  bubble colors, so matching does not depend on color alone.
- **Glyph Racer** — change between three lanes with Left/Right or A/D,
  dodge obstacles and collect plus-shaped energy. Speed rises gradually;
  each generated obstacle row leaves escape lanes open.

Snake, Tris against AI, Pong and Asteroids remain available from the same menu.

## One visual language

All six games share clean geometry, a dot-matrix HUD and the active theme.
Player accents, including Snake, immediately follow your selected palette.
Bubble colors use theme accent and semantic colors with distinct symbols.
Best scores stay on your machine; no accounts, telemetry or cloud services.

## Smoother and lighter

- The arcade engine loads only after you select a game.
- Cached background dots and suspended idle rendering avoid unnecessary work.
- Games pause when the window loses focus, the document is hidden or the
  arcade scrolls out of view. P resumes real-time games.
- Small physics steps improve fast-object collision reliability.
- Resizing preserves the logical field; finished game pieces remain visible.
- Pong paddle crossings, a full Snake board and small Tris boards are handled
  correctly. Grid-divider clicks no longer place a Tris mark.

## Validation

TypeScript, the frontend build, the startup bundle budget and the full frontend
test suite passed, including 22 focused arcade tests. The games
and menu were visually checked in the browser preview, including a yellow
palette. Native desktop gameplay remains to be confirmed. The earlier full
local Go test run was interrupted on a blocked Git-package test; it is not
reported as a complete pass.
